# Page Copy-Direction Pattern Bank

Fill-in structures with one worked example each. These are copy *directions* for wireframes, not final copy.

## Hero Headline Patterns

- **Outcome, their words:** "[Result the visitor wants] — without [the cost they fear]" — *"Know exactly what your restaurant made this month — without touching a spreadsheet."*
- **Kill the objection:** "[Outcome], even if [the disqualifier they believe]" — *"A website that brings in clients, even if you can't write about yourself."*
- **The specific promise:** "[Deliverable] in [timeframe], [differentiator]" — *"Your books closed by the 5th, every month, by people who know restaurants."*
- **The reframe:** "[You're not X. You're Y.]" — *"You don't need more leads. You need fewer forms between them and you."*
- Never: taglines about the company (*"Crafting Digital Experiences"*) — the visitor's outcome, always.

## Problem-Section Openers (make it "me")

- **The scene:** drop them into the pain moment — *"It's 11pm. Service is done. And now the receipts start."*
- **The list of tells:** 3–4 hyper-specific symptoms the right visitor recognizes — *"POS totals that never match the bank. Invoices in a drawer. No idea if the new menu item is profitable."*
- **The self-blame reframe:** "[It's not that you're bad at X. It's that X was built for someone else.]"

## Mechanism Patterns (the "how it works" turn)

- **3-step path:** connect → we do the specific work → you get the plain-English output.
- **Guide stance (StoryBrand):** "[You run the [their domain]. We run the [your domain].]"
- **The named method:** give the process a name — a named mechanism is more believable than a generic service.

## CTA Button Copy (verb the outcome)

- "Book a free 20-min call" (says the cost: 20 minutes)
- "Get my [deliverable]" — *"Get my homepage teardown"*
- "Start [outcome]" — *"Start ranking"* / *"Start painting"*
- Never: "Submit", "Learn more", "Contact us" as the primary.

## Objection/FAQ Patterns (the last "but")

- "My [situation] is a mess / years behind" → "We do catch-up."
- "I can't afford it" → compare against the cost of the problem, not against zero.
- "I already use [tool/provider]" → "We work with it."
- "Will this work for [my sub-case]?" → name the sub-cases you actually serve.
- "What happens after I [CTA]?" → describe the first step plainly; kill the fear of a sales trap.

## Final-CTA Bookends

- Echo the hero's promise, address them at the decision: *"Get back to the kitchen. We'll handle the books."*
- Restate what the action costs and what they leave with: *"20 minutes. No pitch. You'll walk away knowing the one thing to fix in your books."*
